The money basics

Understanding credit

Credit can help you work towards big goals, but it helps to know how it works before you borrow. Let’s break it down, no stress.

So, what is credit?

Credit is money you borrow now and agree to pay back over time. The person or company lending you the money is the lender; you’re the borrower. The original amount you borrow is the principal.

You’ll usually also pay interest—the cost of borrowing— and sometimes fees. Your agreement sets out the repayments and when they’re due, so check the full cost, not just the monthly amount.

In everyday terms: credit lets you pay for something over time, but you’ll pay back what you borrowed plus any interest and fees in your agreement.

Basically, it’s not free money, hey. Future-you still has to pay it back.

What does “credit health” mean?

Your credit health is the picture of how you manage borrowing and repayments over time. A credit history is a record of credit accounts and how they’ve been handled. A credit report is the information a credit bureau keeps about that history. A credit score, where provided, is one way information in a report may be summarised.

Lenders can use credit information along with other details—like your income, expenses, and existing debt—when assessing an application. Different lenders may assess things differently, and a score by itself doesn’t guarantee approval.

Think of your credit history as your track record: it helps show how you’ve handled credit before. It’s not the whole story, but it can be part of the conversation when you apply.

Why might credit matter to you?

Paying for a big goal all at once isn’t always realistic. Credit can spread the cost, but a lender will want to decide whether the repayments are affordable and whether the application fits its criteria.

Thinking about a car?

Car finance

Say you need a car to get to work, campus, or your side hustle. With car finance, a lender may look at your credit history, income, expenses, deposit, and other debt before deciding whether to offer finance and on what terms. A steady repayment record can be useful, but it doesn’t guarantee approval or a particular interest rate.

Eyeing that Jaguar? A healthy credit history can help your application make a stronger impression—but it won’t guarantee approval. The lender still checks affordability and its other criteria. So, before you picture yourself pulling up in Sandton, make sure the monthly instalment fits your real-life budget too.

Home loan (bond)

When you apply for a home loan—often called a bond in South Africa— the lender will assess whether the repayments look affordable. Income, expenses, your deposit, existing debt, and credit information can all matter. Remember to budget for more than the bond repayment too: rates, insurance, and upkeep can add up.

Want your own spot one day? Paying accounts on time and keeping borrowing manageable can help you build a positive track record. It’s one part of the picture; the bank will also look at your income, deposit, expenses, and whether you can afford the bond.

How can you build healthier habits?

  • Know your dates: Keep track of repayment due dates—set a reminder so payday doesn’t catch you sleeping.
  • Borrow within your budget: Leave room for essentials and surprise expenses. Month-end must still month-end.
  • Read the agreement: Check the interest, fees, total repayment, and what happens if you pay late. No nasty surprises, please.
  • Ask early: If you think you’ll miss a payment, contact the lender sooner rather than later and discuss your options.
  • Check your information: Review your credit report when you can and query anything you don’t recognise.
